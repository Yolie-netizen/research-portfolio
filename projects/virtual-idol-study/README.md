# The Influence of Virtual Idols on Teenagers

**Mixed-Methods Research | Completed**

## Overview

This study examines how engagement with virtual idols relates to adolescents' cognitive, emotional, and behavioral outcomes.

The project used a sequential mixed-methods design. Semi-structured interviews were first conducted to explore how adolescents understood and experienced virtual idols. Insights from the qualitative phase were then incorporated into a larger survey study examining several cognitive, emotional, and behavioral outcomes.

## Research Questions

The study examined whether greater engagement with virtual idols was associated with:

- knowledge acquisition;
- emotional experiences;
- imitation behavior;
- excessive consumption; and
- social interaction.

## Research Design

### Qualitative Phase

The project conducted semi-structured interviews with **20 adolescents**, primarily between the ages of 14 and 18.

The interviews explored participants':

- understandings of virtual idols;
- motivations for following them;
- emotional experiences;
- consumption behavior;
- social interaction; and
- perceptions of the positive and negative effects of virtual-idol engagement.

The interview materials were coded and organized around cognitive, emotional, and behavioral themes.

### Quantitative Phase

The survey phase collected **392 responses**, of which **383 were retained as valid responses**.

The questionnaire included measures of:

- virtual-idol engagement;
- imitation behavior;
- excessive consumption;
- social interaction;
- positive and negative emotional experiences;
- knowledge acquisition; and
- attitudes toward virtual idols.

Several multi-item measures were adapted from existing scales.

## Analysis

The quantitative analysis included:

- descriptive statistics;
- reliability analysis;
- correlation analysis; and
- linear regression.

All major multi-item scales reported Cronbach's alpha values above **0.80**, indicating strong internal consistency within the study.

The analysis identified statistically significant relationships between virtual-idol engagement and multiple behavioral, emotional, and cognitive outcomes.

## Key Findings

The study suggests that virtual-idol engagement should not be understood as simply beneficial or harmful.

Greater engagement was associated with positive outcomes such as social interaction, positive emotional experiences, and knowledge acquisition, while also being associated with behaviors such as imitation and excessive consumption.

These findings highlight virtual-idol fandom as a multidimensional social phenomenon involving both opportunities and risks for adolescent participants.
