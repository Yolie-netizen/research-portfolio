# Research Portfolio

I am a Sociology undergraduate at the University of California, Berkeley, with interests in mixed-methods social research, gender and family, digital culture, and quantitative approaches to social questions.

My work centers on sociological research design and analysis, combining qualitative methods such as semi-structured interviews and fieldwork with survey research, statistical analysis, and data visualization. This portfolio presents selected projects with an emphasis on research questions, methodology, and interpretation.

## Selected Projects

### 1. The Influence of Virtual Idols on Teenagers
**Mixed-Methods Research | Completed**

A mixed-methods study examining how engagement with virtual idols relates to teenagers' cognition, emotional experiences, and behavioral intentions.

The project combined **20 semi-structured interviews** with **383 valid survey responses**, followed by qualitative coding, reliability analysis, correlation analysis, and linear regression. The study examined outcomes including social interaction, emotional experience, knowledge acquisition, imitation behavior, and excessive consumption.

### 2. Three Futures: Ideal, Feasible, and Family-Approved
**Qualitative Field Research | Ongoing**

An exploratory case study examining how family expectations, gender norms, geographic mobility, and perceptions of stability shape career planning among a female university student in Kashgar, Xinjiang.

The research uses retrospective career timelines, semi-structured interviews, occupational card sorting, hypothetical job scenarios, and participant validation to compare three imagined futures: the career a participant ideally wants, considers realistically attainable, and believes her family would most approve of.
